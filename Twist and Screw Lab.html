Twist and Screw Lab

Meriam gives a rigid body's velocity as one angular velocity ω plus the velocity of one reference point. Those six numbers are already a twist. This lab shows that any twist is a single screw motion: one spin about one line, plus one slide along that same line. There is one screw, not three.

Meriam
vP = vA + ω × rP/A

Pick a reference point A. ω and vA give the velocity of every point P.

Twist
V = (ω, vA) · 6 numbers

The same two vectors, stacked. The book uses A = a frame's origin: the world origin for Vs, the body origin for Vb.

Screw
axis ŝ through q, pitch h, rate |ω|

Choose A on one special line and vA points along ω. That line is the screw axis.

Every point gets the same slide along the axis, plus ω × (its offset from the axis). Points on the axis keep only the slide.

Things to try, in order

  1. General screw, then watch. Each corner traces a helix, all around the same magenta line and all climbing at the same rate. That is one screw, even though ω has three components.
  2. Move A with its sliders. ω stays fixed and vA changes, but the axis and pitch stay put. The six numbers of the twist depend on where you stand; the screw doesn't.
  3. Press Put A on the screw axis. The violet "around" arrow vanishes, and vA points straight along the axis. The screw axis is exactly the set of points whose velocity is parallel to ω.
  4. Rolling wheel. The pitch is 0 and the axis runs through the contact line under the box. That is Meriam's instant centre of zero velocity, extended into 3-D as a line.
  5. Door hinge. Pitch 0 again, with the axis off to the side. Then nudge vz up: the hinge becomes a screw going into wood.
  6. Set all three ω sliders to 0. The pitch becomes infinite, every point gets the same velocity, and there is no axis to draw. That is a prismatic joint.
